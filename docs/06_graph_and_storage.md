# 6. The dual graph and its storage

> **In one paragraph.** The structural plane is derived exactly from the insights' scopes: a narrower subgroup SPECIALIZES a broader one, two subgroups that differ in one value are SIBLINGs, and overlapping subgroups whose shifts point in opposite directions CONTRAST. Together with the latent plane of [5](05_latent_anchors.md) and a small schema of datasets, batches, dimensions and metrics, this forms one typed graph. Everything is persisted in a workspace folder: append-only journals hold the insights, their vectors and their memberships; the ontology state holds the anchors; the graph snapshot is derived from both. A batch commits atomically behind a checkpoint; a workspace written by another representation version starts degraded until it is reset. Neo4j can mirror the graph; the files remain the source of truth.

**Code** `subgroup_miner/lattice.py`, `insight_graph_service/core/snapshot.py` (the compiler), `graph_query_engine/graph.py` (`DualGraph`, the read model), `insight_contracts/graph.py` (the vocabulary and the snapshot schema), `insight_graph_service/core/workspace.py`, `insight_graph_service/core/uploads.py` (a new version), `insight_graph_service/core/verify.py` (the recomputation), `insight_graph_service/core/chunk_journal.py`, `insight_graph_service/core/fileio.py`, `insight_graph_service/core/neo4j_mirror.py`, `insight_graph_service/core/cypher/`, `insight_graph_service/core/geosparql.py` · **Tests** `tests/test_lattice.py`, `tests/test_persistence.py`, `tests/test_deletion.py`, `tests/test_neo4j_mirror.py`, `tests/test_spatial.py::test_the_graph_exports_as_geosparql` · **Previous** [5. ## 6.1 The structural plane

`structural_edges(insights, config)` reads only the conditions, shifts, targets and supports of the insights of **one dataset** at a time — scopes of different datasets are incomparable — and never an embedding (a test enforces that the module needs no numpy). Let `C(P)` be the condition set of pattern `P`:

| Relation | Rule | Edge, weight | Properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPECIALIZES | `C(B) ⊂ C(A)` strictly and **no present pattern `M` with `C(B) ⊂ C(M) ⊂ C(A)`** — the covering relation (Hasse diagram) of the present patterns | `A → B` (the narrower subgroup specialises the broader), 1.0 | `added_conditions`, `support_ratio = n_A / n_B`, `metric` (the parent's target), `parent_z`, `child_z` |
| SIBLING | `|C(A)| = |C(B)|`, and the two differ in exactly one condition, on the same attribute (same parent scope, another value of one partition attribute) | smaller id → larger id, 1.0 | `parent_scope`, `partition_attribute`, `values` |
| CONTRASTS | `overlap = |C(A) ∩ C(B)| / min(|C(A)|, |C(B)|) ≥ CONTRAST_MIN_OVERLAP` (0.5), and some metric has opposite-signed shifts with `min(|z_A|, |z_B|) ≥ CONTRAST_MIN_SHIFT` (0.5); the strongest such metric is recorded | smaller id → larger id, weight = overlap | `metric`, `z_source`, `z_target`, `scope_overlap`, `relation` ∈ {`specialization_reversal` (a SPECIALIZES pair), `sibling` (a SIBLING pair), `overlap`} |

SPECIALIZES is stored once, child to parent; the walk follows it both ways ([7.3](07_question_answering.md#73-transversal-traversal)).

**The spatial plane.** For geo patterns the commit adds a third plane, derived — never journaled — from each READY geo dataset's `geo.npz` and `covers.npz`, and from each READY table level's `covers.npz` placed by a geo level of its upload, by one pure function, `subgroup_miner.spatial.derive_spatial`, which `Engine._derive` feeds and `build_snapshot` compiles:

| Element | Rule | Weight and properties |
|---|---|---|
| Region `R:<res>:<h3>` | an H3 cell at a resolution of `GEO_REGION_LEVELS` (7, 5, 3, 1) that some pattern is IN_REGION of, and its ancestors at the coarser listed levels; one node for every dataset that reaches it | `resolution`, `h3`, `cells_covered` (the patterns' cells inside it, summed over datasets), `patterns` (its IN_REGION members of both bases) |
| IN_REGION, geo Pattern → Region | at every listed level not finer than the pattern's dataset, its own level included, when `|P ∩ R|` over the smaller of the pattern's cells and the region's occupied cells of that dataset reaches `GEO_REGION_MIN_SHARE` (0.2): a focus inside a region and a pattern covering it both score 1.0 (at its own level a cell is its own region) | the share; `basis: cells`, `share`, `n` (the pattern's cells in the region) |
| IN_REGION, base Pattern → Region | a table level's pattern, placed by the finest READY geo level of its upload with its point columns that holds one row per point (`placed_by`; the columns as the signed profiles resolve them, the geo level's `geo.location` and the table's `points`, so another pair of coordinates of the same file and an edited locator place nothing; a record failing its metadata digest places nothing either ([13.4](13_trust.md#134-integrity)); a matching row count alone links nothing, and a deleted level passes the table to the next). An event lies in its finest cell, and at a listed level in the region holding that cell's centre (`event_regions`). Over the pattern's located events `|P_loc|`, with x of them in R, n_R the map's events there and N all of them: on a level of at least `GEO_LIFT_MIN_REGIONS` (12) occupied regions (holding a located event), `lift = (x / |P_loc|) / (n_R / N) ≥ GEO_LIFT_MIN` (1.5) and q ≤ `GEO_LINK_FDR` (0.05), q the Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment over the level's occupied regions of the binomial tail `P(X ≥ x | n_R, |P_loc| / N)` (where it is small, never below the exact hypergeometric tail at the x a link needs, 5 or more, so never more links — below 5 it can fall under it, by less than 1e-4 —, and hundreds of times faster on a level of thousands of regions); on a level of fewer, `x / |P_loc| ≥ GEO_REGION_MIN_SHARE` and lift ≥ 1 (no rarer there than the map); on both, `x ≥ min(GEO_LINK_MIN_EVENTS (30), max(5, ⌈0.05 · |P_loc|⌉))` (`spatial.link_min_events`, which the map's window applies too). A pattern with fewer than `GEO_LOCATED_MIN` (0.8) of its events located has none (`geo: partial`) | `x / |P_loc|`; `basis: events`, `rule` (`lift` or `share`), `share`, `lift`, `n` (x), `q`, `placed_by` |
| PARENT_OF, Region → Region | a region to its parent at the next coarser listed level | 1.0 |
| CO_LOCATED, Pattern — Pattern | two patterns of one resolution, of any datasets: `overlaps` when their ring-1-dilated extents overlap by `|A ∩ B| / min(|A|, |B|) ≥ GEO_COLOCATION_MIN` (0.3); else `adjacent` when the extents touch or share cells; else `same_region` when both are IN_REGION of one region at the nearest listed level at least two coarser. Each pattern keeps its `COLOCATED_TOP` (3) strongest partners of its own dataset and as many of the other datasets, relation first, then weight; a pair either side keeps is an edge (so a pattern others choose can have more), and a pair the lattice links is left out | `overlaps`: the overlap; `adjacent`: the share of both extents' cells on the common edge (`|(A ∪ ring A) ∩ B| + |(B ∪ ring B) ∩ A|` over `|A| + |B|`, at most 1); `same_region`: the smaller of the two shares; `relation` |
| `compactness` on a geo Pattern | the share of the extent's neighbour links that stay inside it | — |

**A base pattern's place** (`place_table`). A table level's pattern has no cells of its own; its events, through the point columns the table level leaves out, have them. Two rules split the levels because each answers where the other cannot. For a subgroup that has nothing to do with place, its share in a region is the region's share of the map, so the share rule places any large subgroup in a region dense for the whole map: on the calibration export ([2.9](02_discovery.md#29-spatial-datasets-the-geo-option)) one resolution-3 region holds a third of the events, and the share rule gave 43 base edges at that level that lift does not, as it gave one to random subgroups of the rows. Lift asks the region to hold more of the pattern than of the map, and places a random subgroup nowhere; with the five regions of resolution 1 it seldom reaches significance, and share gives the coarse place, where the pattern is no rarer than the map (a region holding most of the map takes a fifth of nearly any pattern: on California 8 of the 19 share edges at resolution 1 had a lift below 1, down to 0.59). `GEO_LIFT_MIN_REGIONS` (12) splits the levels the same way on every measured set (5 regions at resolution 1, 46–93 at 3, hundreds at 5). The share is over the located events (a located event is one the geo level holds a point for), so with `GEO_LOCATED_MIN` (0.8) it differs from the share over all the pattern's events by at most a fifth; the bound is that of `GEO_FOOTPRINT_SHARE`. Its `share` is of its events, a geo pattern's of the region's cells: the two do not compare, and a base edge has passed its rule already. A pattern's regions at two levels need not nest by PARENT_OF, since each level takes the region holding the cell's centre (PARENT_OF follows the H3 hierarchy), so the console draws a pattern's places at every level it lies at, not their PARENT_OF ancestors (`shown_regions`, [8.7](08_interface.md#87-the-map)), and a region's cell rows on the map are the cells whose centre it holds (`region_features`), as the events are placed. A base pattern is never CO_LOCATED, is in neither `places` nor `occupied` (its credibility stays phase A, rule `anchor`), and is no step of a scale chain (it has no resolution); its IN_REGION are read like any other by `picture` and the regions' `patterns`. The measured counts are in [10.3](10_verification.md#103-hypothesis-benchmark).

The structural plane stays within one dataset; CO_LOCATED and the regions join any datasets, since an H3 cell means the same place in every one of them. IN_REGION and PARENT_OF are not walked. A region holds a finer dataset's cells by their centres — each cell lies in the coarse cell holding its centre, as the points were indexed (`event_regions`); a centre on a coarse boundary, which the platforms' floating point would decide (on California at 5, 114 of the 884 cells two levels coarser), goes by one rule everywhere: to its H3 parent when the parent is among the cells `CENTRE_TIE_DEG` (1e-7°) around the centre, else to the least of them (`centre_region`) —, and a dataset at the region's own resolution holds its points' own cells; one rule for the geo patterns' IN_REGION and `cells_covered`, the base patterns' events, the overlay's cell rows, credibility's places ([6.2](#62-the-graph-schema)) and a map zone ([7.2](07_question_answering.md#72-seeds)). PARENT_OF follows the H3 hierarchy of the regions themselves, and H3 children do not tile their parent: one or two levels coarser every cell lies in its parent (two levels up, a centre outside it is always on a boundary), from three levels on a few percent do not (on California at 8, 627 of 12,333 cells at 5), so along region borders a pattern's regions at two levels need not nest by PARENT_OF, and a region's PARENT_OF ancestor need not be the coarse region its cells lie in; a finding well inside its region meets its coarser level either way. (Until #105, a geo pattern's IN_REGION followed `cell_to_parent`: with the centre, of the 3,265 geo IN_REGION edges of a points upload at 8, 7 and table 15 went and 18 came, of California's 6,597 at 6, 5 and table 29 went and 36 came; CO_LOCATED, the scale labels and the insights stayed the same.) `derive_spatial` also gives each pattern's and each dataset's cells at its own resolution and the cells holding their centres at every listed level coarser (`places`, `occupied`), which credibility reads (below) and no node stores.

Because conditions are closed intents ([2.3](02_discovery.md#23-deduplication-before-validation)), this is the Hasse diagram of a concept lattice restricted to the present patterns: a strictly smaller extent always has a strictly larger intent, and SPECIALIZES follows extent containment exactly. The EDA enumerates 2- and 3-conjunctions, but implied conditions can make intents longer.

> **Running example.** `P-bc4657a04746` (phones ∧ US) is specialised by its three channel refinements (`phones ∧ US ∧ online`, `∧ retail`, `∧ partner`, each `added_conditions: ["channel=…"]`), has two SIBLINGs (phones in the other regions) and contrasts with nothing. A typical contrast on the demo is `laptops ∧ EU: margin +1.13 sd` ↔ `laptops ∧ EU ∧ retail: margin −0.86 sd`, a `specialization_reversal` with weight 1.0 — the planted contrasting subgroup.

## 6.2 The graph schema

`snapshot.build_snapshot(records=, activations=, vectors=, batches=, representation=, ontology=, settings=)` (`insight_graph_service/core/snapshot.py`) assembles the snapshot from the journal records, activations and vectors, the batch records (a READY one carries its dataset's profile), the recorded vector contract and the ontology — it reads no file; `Engine` gathers these from the workspace. The node and edge vocabulary, the node ids and `SNAPSHOT_VERSION` belong to the shared kernel (`insight_contracts/graph.py`). `DualGraph(snapshot)` (`graph_query_engine/graph.py`) is the read-only adjacency index that traversal, evidence and the console use; it refuses a snapshot of another version.

| Node | Id | Label | Key properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pattern | `P-<hash>` | the headline ([8.5](08_interface.md#85-text-shown-to-people)) | the journal record unchanged: `Insight` fields + `row_id`, `canonical`, `embedding`, `record_digest`; plus `integrity`, computed at every compile: `ok` when the record's statistics match its digest, `mismatch` when they do not, `unverified` for a record journaled without one; `credibility` and `credibility_basis`, computed at every compile from the other sources (below); a geo pattern's `compactness` and a base pattern's `geo: partial` ([6.1](#61-the-structural-plane)) |
| Attractor | `A-<k>` | the anchor label | [5.8](05_latent_anchors.md#58-how-an-anchor-is-described) |
| Dimension | `D:<dataset>:<column>` | raw column name | `name, cardinality, entropy` — the EDA-selected dimensions plus every attribute a closed intent uses |
| Metric | `M:<dataset>:<column>` | `humanize(name)` | `name, global_median, global_mad`; `indicator` and `unit` when the dataset's indicator registry names the metric ([2.1](02_discovery.md#21-ingestion)) |
| Dataset | `DS:<dataset_id>` | file name | `filename, rows, columns`, `reliability` (`A`–`F` from the batch record, `F` when undeclared), `source` when declared ([2.1](02_discovery.md#21-ingestion)), `upload_id`, `level` and `upload_levels` (the levels of every batch record of that `upload_id`, of any status) for an HTTP upload ([2.9](02_discovery.md#29-spatial-datasets-the-geo-option)), `supersedes` when it names the version it replaced ([6.8](#68-deleting-a-dataset)); `integrity`, computed at every compile from the record's `metadata_digest` (`ok`, `mismatch`, `unverified` for a record written without one; below). `Engine.update_dataset_metadata` (`PATCH /api/datasets/{id}`) rewrites them, and the indicator registry, on the READY batch record, signs it again and recompiles the snapshot: no new batch, no mining, the same pattern ids |
| Batch | `B:<batch_id>` | batch id | `batch_seq, created_at, status` |

**Credibility** (`snapshot.credibility`; the Admiralty information-credibility scale operationalised, not defined: why, in [13.1](13_trust.md#131-the-decision)). Every pattern gets `credibility ∈ {1, 2, 5, 6}` and `credibility_basis {rule, sources, patterns, comparability?, confirmed_by?}`, `rule` `anchor` (phase A) for a pattern without a place and `place` (phase B) for a geo pattern, from the other datasets of the graph, never from its own p, stability or weight. Another *source* is another declared `source` (compared casefolded, so «Field reports» and «field reports» are one) outside the pattern's own dataset and upload: the levels of one upload are one source, and so are a version and the one it `supersedes` ([6.8](#68-deleting-a-dataset)) while both are in the graph. Indicators and units compare casefolded too, a unit only when both give one (`price` in UAH is not `price` in USD); `sources` lists each source once. Metrics compare through the indicator registry (`indicators`, [2.1](02_discovery.md#21-ingestion)); a phenomenon shift is one the prompt shows (`phenomenon_shifts`).

| Level | When | `comparability` |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | a pattern of another declared source shifts a metric of the same indicator materially the opposite way in the same subgroup (one pattern's conditions contain the other's); wins over 1, whose patterns then stand in `confirmed_by` | `indicator` |
| 1 | a pattern of another declared source shares a non-weak anchor and shifts a metric of the same indicator the same way | `indicator` |
| 2 | another dataset, declared or not (not of the same upload or source), shifts a metric of the same name | `nominal` |
| 6 | none of these | — |

Phase B compares a geo pattern only with the geo patterns of other sources at its place: its cells against theirs at the finest resolution both datasets have, their own or a listed level (two datasets at resolution 8 compare their cells, one at 8 and one at 5 meet at 5). At a coarser level a place is the set of cells holding its cells' centres (`spatial.places`, `occupied`), where a dataset of that resolution indexed the same points, not their H3 parents, which differ for about 6 % of cells; each coarse cell counts once however many of the finer cells lie in it, so a finer place that only touches a coarse one meets it there. Cells, not IN_REGION, since a region holding one cell of a sparse pattern would count it whole. The rules of the table hold with the place in the subgroup's stead:

| Level | When (phase B) | `comparability` |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | as above, over a comparable place: the shared cells reach `GEO_REGION_MIN_SHARE` (0.2) of the larger of the two places, in any subgroup | `indicator` |
| 1 | as above, where the shared cells reach that share of the smaller place (one lies mostly inside the other) | `indicator` |
| 2 | another dataset, declared or not, has a pattern there with a metric of the same name, or events in that share of the place's cells (`occupied`) | `nominal` |
| 6 | no other source has events there | — |

A contradiction needs comparable places because the average over a wide place says nothing about a spot inside it: on S-poison a broad pattern of ordinary cells that holds the planted spot shifts `load` the other way, as each source measures from its own baseline ([10.3](10_verification.md#103-hypothesis-benchmark)). A pattern without a place, such as one of a table level, stays in phase A, so a geo pattern of another source can confirm it from anywhere, while a geo pattern itself is confirmed by geo patterns only.

So two undeclared datasets, or two without a registry, never give 1 or 5, and a tampered pattern (`integrity: mismatch`) confirms nothing. The level changes when another dataset arrives or goes; `credibility_basis` says why, and no history is kept. Phase A sees no place: a phenomenon that recurs in another source anywhere confirms; phase B confirms only at the place, so the focus the second source of S-two-sources does not have, nor has events near, is 6, where phase A would give 1 through another focus ([10.3](10_verification.md#103-hypothesis-benchmark)). A contradiction needs the same metric the other way in the same subgroup, not an opposite phenomenon vector: across a real pair of sources the phenomenon-block cosine spans −0.99 to 0.98 ([10.3](10_verification.md#103-hypothesis-benchmark), the second-source spike). The evidence shows the level and its basis on the item's `trust:` line ([7.4](07_question_answering.md#74-the-evidence-object)). Limits: a covariance insight has no phenomenon shift, so it is always 6; conditions compare as written (`attribute=value`) across datasets, while metrics compare through the registry, so a quantile band such as `q4` of two datasets may cover different ranges; a shift is measured from its own dataset's baseline, so two broad patterns of differently composed sources can contradict over one place; one phenomenon learned by two anchors, one per source, confirms nothing (2), in either phase; two fine sources compare cell by cell, so the same focus shifted by more than half its radius in one of them is 2, not 1; against a coarse source a fine spot is compared at the coarse resolution, where a place of a few cells is comparable with the spot and can contradict it; a tampered pattern gets a level of its own but confirms nothing and never reaches the evidence.

| Edge | From → to | Plane | Weight and properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPECIALIZES, SIBLING, CONTRASTS | Pattern → Pattern | structural | [6.1](#61-the-structural-plane) |
| CO_LOCATED, IN_REGION, PARENT_OF | Pattern — Pattern, Pattern → Region, Region → Region | spatial | [6.1](#61-the-structural-plane) |
| ACTIVATES | Pattern → Attractor | bridge | `alignment` with the **current** centroid (also the weight); `strength`, `insight_weight`, `engine_weight`, `alignment_at_ingest`, `source`, `batch_id`, `weak` — the membership was rerouted below `MIN_ACTIVATION_ALIGNMENT` at ingest, or its current alignment is below that floor; it counts for coverage and is not walked. `source: co_membership` marks a membership compiled with the assignment rule ([5.7](05_latent_anchors.md#57-links-between-anchors)) |
| RELATED_TO | Attractor → Attractor (smaller → larger id) | latent | mutual-kNN cosine; `kind: mutual_knn` |
| CO_OCCURS | Attractor → Attractor (smaller → larger id) | latent | `min(1, Σ_p W[p, j]·W[p, k])` over shared non-weak members ([5.7](05_latent_anchors.md#57-links-between-anchors)); `kind: co_occurrence`, `shared` |
| HAS_SCOPE | Pattern → Dimension | schema | 1.0; `value` |
| TARGETS | Pattern → Metric | schema | `min(1, |z| / 3)` for the target and every shift with `|z| ≥ MIN_COMPONENT_Z` (`PhenomenonThresholds.material`); `role` primary / secondary, `z`, medians |
| DISCOVERED_IN | Pattern → Batch | provenance | 1.0 |
| OF_DATASET | Batch → Dataset | provenance | 1.0 |

Edge ids are `<TYPE>:<source>-><target>`. Ids are dataset-scoped, because datasets reuse column names with different meanings. Activations that point to an unjournaled pattern or a dead anchor are skipped when the snapshot is built.

> **Running example.** The demo snapshot (hashing double, `tests/golden/`) holds 28 Pattern, 8 Attractor and the schema nodes, and 282 edges: SPECIALIZES 24, SIBLING 37, CONTRASTS 18, ACTIVATES 45, RELATED_TO 8, CO_OCCURS 2, HAS_SCOPE 70, TARGETS 49, DISCOVERED_IN 28, OF_DATASET 1. It is not a geo dataset, so it has no spatial plane.

## 6.3 The workspace on disk

`WORKSPACE_DIR` (default `workspace/`) holds one knowledge base. `workspace.Workspace` owns the layout — no other module builds a path below the workspace root; the vendored `ChunkJournal` (`insight_graph_service/core/chunk_journal.py`, constructed with `records_name="patterns.jsonl"`) owns the journal files.

**Atomic replacement.** Batch records, the snapshot and the other JSON files, the blocks files and the vector matrix are written beside the target and renamed over it (`fileio.replace_file`, which removes the temporary file if the rename fails). Readers in the service never open a file the writer replaces: the batch registry and the vector contract are read once by `Workspace` and written through (`save_batch`, `record_representation`; reloaded after a rollback), and everything a question reads is on the committed state ([6.4](#64-commit-rollback-and-recovery)). A second process reading the same workspace (a read-only engine) sees the files as of its start.

The snapshot is **derived data**: every commit regenerates it from the journals, the registry (each READY record carries its dataset's profile) and the concept store, recomputing ACTIVATES alignments against the current centroids, compiling the co-memberships and CO_OCCURS links ([5.7](05_latent_anchors.md#57-links-between-anchors)) and RELATED_TO from the topology; an engine that starts on a snapshot of another `SNAPSHOT_VERSION` regenerates it the same way ([6.4](#64-commit-rollback-and-recovery)). Raw rows are stored only in the uploaded file under `uploads/`, which stays until the dataset's deletion or a reset.

## 6.4 Commit, rollback and recovery

**Commit order** (stages UPDATING_ONTOLOGY, BUILDING_GRAPH and PERSISTING of `Engine.process`):

```text
transaction: checkpoint + marker pending.json → ontology ingest → pattern records (+ provenance.rows_ref) and
covers.npz → duplicate guard (duplicate_patterns if a pattern id is already journaled) → journal append (records,
activations, vectors, blocks) → ConceptStore.save → record_representation → batch sequence → snapshot build + atomic
write → committed graph and frame loaded → batch saved READY (the commit point) → marker removed, checkpoint discarded
then: readers switched to the new graph and frame → optional Neo4j sync (a failure is a warning)
```

**One transaction protocol.** A batch commit, a dataset deletion ([6.8](#68-deleting-a-dataset)) and a change of a dataset's metadata (`PATCH`: source, reliability, indicators; [6.2](#62-the-graph-schema)) each run in `Workspace.transaction(intent)`: it checkpoints, then writes `pending.json` holding the intent (`batch_id` or `dataset_id`) and the checkpoint. `checkpoint()` copies `state/` and `graph/snapshot.json` to `checkpoint/` and stores `ChunkJournal.mark()` (the sizes of both logs and the record count); a deletion, which shrinks the journal, passes `journal=True` and the journal folder is copied too. An exception in the body rolls back — `rollback()` truncates the journal to the mark (or restores the copied folder), restores `state/`, puts the previous snapshot back (or removes it when there was none) and returns any moved-aside files — then removes the marker and the checkpoint. A rollback that fails itself is logged and leaves both: the batch is recorded FAILED with the warning `rollback failed`, and every later transaction is refused (`rollback_pending`) until a writer restart retries it, so nothing can overwrite the only good copy. A marker that cannot be removed after the body (a file another process holds open) depends on the write's commit point: a batch has committed at its READY record, so it stays READY and is published, the marker is logged and left, later transactions are refused (`rollback_pending`) and the next writer start closes it; a deletion or a metadata change commits when its marker goes, so it fails, readers keep the previous state and the next writer start rolls it back. The READY record is a batch's commit point, so a failure to write it — a full disk, a permission error — rolls the batch back completely: readers never see a snapshot whose patterns the journal does not hold. Files outside the journal, the state and the snapshot survive a failed batch — `journal/blocks/<batch>.npz`, `covers.npz`, `geo.npz`, `cells.geojson`, `profile.json`, `rejections.json` and the upload; nothing reads them for a batch that is not READY, and the next ingest of that dataset overwrites them.

**Committed caches.** `Engine.graph()` and `Engine.frame()` (`LatentFrame`: pattern id → unit vector, anchor id → centroid) are loaded inside the transaction, before the commit point, and switched in together after it — so a committed batch cannot fail afterwards, and readers never see a half-appended journal or a half-saved ontology. Readers get all three through one object, `Engine.committed()` → `CommittedState(graph, frame, catalog)`, loaded when the engine starts and replaced whole by every commit (a reset replaces it with the empty workspace's), so no reader mixes two commits or waits for a lock. What readers derive from it — the console's graph elements, the map's overlay, projection and window, an insight's places and scale chain — is memoised on its graph (`DualGraph.memoised`, one build per key however many threads ask), so a commit's new graph starts with none of it and a reader still holding the old graph keeps its own. The literal catalog, and the vector of any pattern stored without one, are filled when the state is built — at the commit, on the batch thread, or when the engine starts — so a question only reads; a writer rewrites the literal cache whenever the literal set changed, so a deleted dataset's values leave it. An engine that starts on a snapshot of another `SNAPSHOT_VERSION` rebuilds it from the journals (a writer also saves it; a read-only engine keeps the rebuild in memory); on journals of another canonical or representation version it starts degraded ([6.5](#65-versions-degraded-start-and-reset)).

**One writer per workspace.** A *writer* engine (`Engine(settings)`, i.e. `recover=True`: the graph service, and the measurement scripts on their own scratch workspaces) first takes the workspace's exclusive writer lock on `<workspace>.writer.lock`, a file beside the workspace folder (so a reset can delete the folder while it is held) that also records the holder's pid: on Windows `msvcrt` locks one byte (at offset 1 MiB, so the pid stays readable), on POSIX `fcntl.flock` locks the whole file. The lock is held until the process exits and released by the operating system, also after a crash; it is re-entrant within a process. A second writer process gets `WorkspaceBusy` — the graph service logs it and exits with code 2 — instead of interleaving batches with the first or touching its queue. A read-only engine (`recover=False`) takes no lock, recovers nothing, changes no batch, journal or ontology state and saves no derived file (an outdated snapshot is rebuilt in memory only). Within the writer, batches run one at a time (a re-entrant lock; the graph service processes batches on a single worker thread).

**Recovery.** Holding the lock, the writer first finishes an interrupted transaction (`Workspace.recover`): a batch whose READY record was written had committed and keeps its writes; anything else — a batch before its commit point, any deletion — is rolled back from the checkpoint in the marker. `rollback()` is repeatable, so a rollback that failed halfway is completed. Then every unfinished batch fails (`interrupted`) — with no other writer alive, any batch that is not terminal was left by a process that died, including uploads that were still queued. A recovery that fails itself does not stop the start: the unfinished batches still fail, so a reset is never refused for a batch no process runs, and the engine starts degraded ([6.5](#65-versions-degraded-start-and-reset)).

**Idempotency.** The dataset id is content-addressed, so a READY batch for the same id makes a new upload `SKIPPED` (`duplicate_of`); pattern ids are deterministic; a Neo4j publish is idempotent (MERGE on ids, then the same stale deletions). A failed batch keeps its `batch_seq`, and the next batch reuses that number.

## 6.5 Versions, degraded start and reset

A workspace records the `EmbeddingSpec` of its first batch ([4.6](04_representation.md#46-representation-identity-and-versions)). Vectors of different representations never meet:

* **At start**, `Workspace.check_versions` compares the stored canonical and representation versions with the code's (`CANONICAL_VERSION`, `REPRESENTATION_VERSION`) without loading a model. A workspace of another version — or one whose recovery failed ([6.4](#64-commit-rollback-and-recovery)), or whose state cannot be read (the journals, or a file the snapshot is derived from: a READY geo level's `geo.npz` or `covers.npz`, a placed table level's `covers.npz`) — **starts degraded**: the service starts, the engine serves the empty state, `Engine.problem` says why (`GET /api/health` reports it as `problem`), and every guarded call — `upload`, `submit`, `search`, `evidence`, `delete_dataset`, the map's reads — is refused with the code `workspace_degraded`: `POST /api/upload`, `POST /api/demo`, `POST /api/search` and `DELETE /api/datasets/{id}` answer with the status of [12.4](12_architecture.md#124-services-and-contracts). A degraded start never syncs the Neo4j mirror ([6.6](#66-neo4j-mirror)), so the mirror keeps the last good graph.
* **At ingestion and for every question**, `Workspace.check_representation` compares the fingerprint: a batch under another fingerprint fails with `representation_mismatch` before it touches the journal or the ontology, and `POST /api/search` answers 409 with `code: representation_mismatch`.
* **A snapshot** of another `SNAPSHOT_VERSION` is only derived data: the engine rebuilds it from the journals at start ([6.3](#63-the-workspace-on-disk)).

**Reset** (`POST /api/reset`, `Engine.reset`) is the way out. It renames the workspace folder aside in one step — a failure leaves it whole — and deletes it (a leftover `<name>.deleting-*` folder is safe to remove; the writer lock beside it stays held), opens the empty workspace, which clears `problem`, and clears the Neo4j mirror when it is enabled. It is refused with `busy` (409), never queued, while a batch, a deletion or a metadata change runs or an upload waits. This proof-of-concept does not migrate a workspace to a new version: after the reset the data are ingested again, and the same file with the same options gets the same dataset id (content + options).

## 6.6 Neo4j mirror

With `NEO4J_ENABLED=true`, every publish makes `NEO4J_DATABASE` equal to the snapshot (`neo4j_mirror.publish_snapshot`):

* before the write, each as its own statement: `CREATE DATABASE <db> IF NOT EXISTS WAIT` (errors ignored: an edition without multi-database support, or a user without the right, uses the database as configured), then unique constraints on all seven labels (`cypher/ensure_constraints.cypher`);
* in one write transaction, parameterised `UNWIND … MERGE (n:Label {id}) SET n = props, n.id = id` (the props include the node's `label`) and `MERGE (s)-[r:TYPE]->(t) SET r = props, r.weight = …`, batched by `NEO4J_LOAD_BATCH_SIZE` — properties are **replaced**, so a property that disappeared or became `None` is removed (`None` values are never written);
* then every node of the seven labels whose id the snapshot does not hold is deleted with its relationships, every relationship of the `EDGE_ENDPOINTS` types whose (source, target) pair it does not hold is deleted, and so is every relationship between SIG nodes of a type the schema no longer has (`STALE_TYPES_QUERY`) — stale anchors, links, patterns and types never linger;
* nested properties are stored as JSON strings in `<key>_json` (`conditions_json`, `shifts_json`, `canonical_json`, `signature_json`, `centroid_json`, …).

Publishing happens after every READY batch and after every dataset deletion; `POST /api/reset` publishes an empty snapshot, which clears the mirror. When the graph service starts, `Engine.startup_sync` publishes the committed snapshot once — a commit made while Neo4j was down reaches it — but only from a healthy, non-empty workspace: a degraded or empty start never wipes the mirror. It runs on the batch worker thread under the engine's write lock, so it never interleaves with a batch's, a deletion's or a reset's own sync. SIG owns its seven labels in that database: one workspace per database, and no other data under those labels. A Neo4j failure leaves the batch READY with `neo4j.status = failed` and the warning `graph persistence (Neo4j) failed`; a deletion and a reset report it in their response (`neo4j.status`) and go on. `insight_graph_service/core/cypher/queries/transversal.cypher` approximates the traversal of [7.3](07_question_answering.md#73-transversal-traversal) for the Browser: the same walkable edges, one latent hop and one lattice hop, the best path per target, but no budgeted best-first search.

## 6.7 Configuration and guarantees

| Parameter | Default |
|---|---|
| `WORKSPACE_DIR` | `workspace` |
| `CONTRAST_MIN_OVERLAP`, `CONTRAST_MIN_SHIFT` | 0.5, 0.5 |
| `NEO4J_ENABLED`, `NEO4J_URI`, `NEO4J_USER`, `NEO4J_PASSWORD`, `NEO4J_DATABASE`, `NEO4J_LOAD_BATCH_SIZE` | false, `bolt://localhost:7687`, `neo4j`, "", `sigv1`, 5000 |

Guarantees (tests: `test_lattice.py`; `test_persistence.py`: write → reload → rebuild equality, idempotent re-ingest, graph consistency, rollback after a simulated failure, a failed rollback that blocks writes until recovered, representation mismatch, a batch killed after its journal append and recovered, a reset refused while a batch runs, a second writer process refused while the first keeps its queue, an outdated workspace that starts degraded — writes and questions refused, no mirror sync at its start — and is usable again after a reset, a failed recovery that starts degraded, a batch that stays READY when a post-commit save fails; `test_deletion.py`: a failed READY save that rolls back the snapshot too; `test_integrity.py`: a journal number changed after the append caught at the rebuild and a record without a digest read as unverified; `test_neo4j_mirror.py`: the Neo4j mirror equal to the snapshot — replaced properties, stale nodes and relationships deleted, an empty snapshot clearing it — against a fake driver):

* No transitive SPECIALIZES edges; structural edges connect patterns of one dataset, CO_LOCATED patterns of one resolution in any datasets; RELATED_TO and CO_OCCURS connect only anchors; the latent plane has at most `RELATED_TO_PEER_COUNT · N / 2` RELATED_TO edges, and every CO_OCCURS endpoint has a non-weak member.
* `journal rows == vector rows == ConceptStore.next_chunk_id`; snapshot ids are identical after reload and after rebuild.
* `READY` implies that journals, state and snapshot are mutually consistent; no transaction starts while `pending.json` exists.
* A field changed in `journal/patterns.jsonl` after the append makes its pattern `integrity: mismatch` at the next compile (a commit, or a start: every start rebuilds the snapshot, so its integrity and credibility are never read back from the file). Such a pattern is never a seed and never an evidence item ([7.4](07_question_answering.md#74-the-evidence-object)), and it does not describe its anchors: their labels, signatures and counts are computed from the other members ([5.8](05_latent_anchors.md#58-how-an-anchor-is-described)). The limits:
  * The digest is an unkeyed SHA-256. It catches an edit that leaves it stale (by hand, or by a tool that rewrites numbers), not a writer who recomputes it.
  * It guards the journal, not the snapshot file, which is derived and rebuilt from it.
  * It does not cover `provenance` (only its spatial ingest fields), so the file name a citation footer shows can be edited unnoticed.
  * It does not cover the spatial plane, which is derived at every commit (`compactness`, regions, CO_LOCATED).
  * A walk may still pass through a tampered pattern: its id can appear in another item's path. It takes no slot of the walk's budget ([7.3](07_question_answering.md#73-transversal-traversal)) and no place in a region's `picture`.
  * The map endpoints serve the journal as stored, and the map files (`geo.npz`, `cells.geojson`, written once with the batch) are signed by no digest and not read by the recomputation: a map file edited by hand is drawn as edited. `cells.geojson` is display data the server only serves: the console draws it and takes a zone's H3 ids from it, which the server places on `geo.npz` ([13.4](13_trust.md#134-integrity)).
* Every insight can be recomputed from its stored upload (`Engine.verify_pattern`, `POST /api/patterns/{id}/verify`, [8.1](08_interface.md#81-http-api)): the file is read anew with its batch's options (`Engine._load`, as its processing read it, under the current miner settings; never the parse an upload's levels share), a dataset id other than its own is `source_changed` and a missing file `source_missing`. `subgroup_miner.discovery.recompute_statistics` selects the rows its conditions meet on that table (and those its EDA selector meets) and computes over them the support, every shift's medians, MAD and robust z, the primary metric's median test with the same effective n, the adjusted p over its dataset's test family and a geo insight's spatial provenance. `verify_diff` then checks the record's structure against them — its id against its conditions, its selector's rows, `covers.npz` and `row_hash` against the rows, its target as its first shift, the one whose median test its p and spatial provenance are (on a covariance insight, the stronger metric of its pair, as selection picks it), a missing p only on a covariance insight, its `multiple_testing_family` against the one its dataset's records share — and every number within a relative 1e-9 (`reproduced`), or lists what does not match (`differs`). This catches what the digest cannot: a number or a structure forged with its digest recomputed. Not checked (`NOT_RECOMPUTED`): which metrics shift, since the mining is not re-run (a shift left out of the record passes); whether the insight is a covariance one, which reads the bootstrap stability (a shift insight relabelled covariance, its p dropped, passes when its target is also the stronger metric of its pair); the bootstrap stability and the scores and weight that read it, and a test family forged in every record of the dataset at once. A change of a miner setting that shapes the table (`MIN_ROWS`, `MIN_CELL_POINTS`) after the ingest reads as `source_changed` or `differs` for a faithful record. The check takes no writer lock: a file removed meanwhile reads `source_missing`, and the rows come from the conditions, not from `covers.npz`: a pattern without stored covers is still checked, its rows named in `not_checked`.
* What the graph reads of a dataset in its batch record (`BATCH_DIGEST_FIELDS`: `dataset_id, upload_id, level, filename, source, reliability, indicators, supersedes, profile`) is signed as `metadata_digest` (`snapshot.metadata_digest`, over the fields as the record is stored) when the batch turns READY and at every `PATCH`. A record changed past them is `integrity: mismatch` on its Dataset at the next compile: its declared source, reliability, indicator registry and glossary are ignored, so it is undeclared for credibility and gives no 1 or 5; its trust lines say the metadata failed the check, the summary tags it `джерело невідоме` and a `NOTE` names the dataset ([7.4](07_question_answering.md#74-the-evidence-object)); its patterns stay, their statistics signed apart. A `PATCH` refuses such a record (`metadata_mismatch`) instead of signing it. Limits: the digest is unkeyed, as the record digest is; the profile's baselines it covers are still shown; what ties the dataset to others (`upload_id`, `level`, `supersedes`) is kept, which can only make sources less independent (a 2 that becomes 6); a record whose digest is removed reads `unverified`, as one written before digests does, and is used as declared, its trust lines marked `metadata unverified`.

## 6.8 Deleting a dataset

`Engine.delete_dataset(dataset_id)` (`DELETE /api/datasets/{id}`, the card's *Delete* button; the id may also be the batch id of an upload that failed before it had a dataset id) removes one dataset from the knowledge base while the rest stays intact — the counterpart of the reset, which removes everything. It is a writer operation under the engine lock, refused with `busy` at once while any batch or another write runs, and while one of the dataset's batches is still to run, with `unknown_dataset` (HTTP 404) when no batch carries that id, and with `workspace_degraded` on a degraded workspace. `Engine.delete_upload(upload_id)` (`DELETE /api/uploads/{id}`; `unknown_upload`, HTTP 404) removes every dataset the upload's levels made or found already ingested, in the same one transaction. An upload file stays while a batch of another level still points at it. It is all or nothing:

```text
transaction (6.4): checkpoint (state, journal, snapshot) + marker pending.json → datasets/<id>/,
journal/blocks/<batch>.npz, the batch records (READY, FAILED and SKIPPED alike) and the upload moved into the
checkpoint → ontology.forget: masses recounted, anchors that lost a member moved, orphan anchors dropped (5.11) → the journal rewritten without
the dataset's rows (row ids renumbered in journal order; next_chunk_id = rows) → state saved → snapshot rebuilt and
saved → committed graph and frame loaded → marker removed (the commit point) → checkpoint discarded (this deletes
the moved files)
then, as after a batch: readers switched → Neo4j synced
```

Dataset-specific entities (patterns, memberships, blocks, covers, profile, records, the upload) always go; the shared entities — anchors and their links — follow the orphan rule of [5.11](05_latent_anchors.md#511-removing-patterns-the-orphan-rule). Structural edges need no treatment: they are derived from the remaining patterns when the snapshot is rebuilt, and the Neo4j publish deletes whatever the snapshot no longer holds ([6.6](#66-neo4j-mirror)). An exception before the commit point rolls everything back at once — journal, state, snapshot, and the moved files go back where they were. A crash before it leaves the marker: the next writer start (`Workspace.recover`) rolls back the same way, so the journal and the ontology can never be left disagreeing. After the commit point a crash only leaves the checkpoint folder behind, which the next checkpoint replaces. A deleted dataset can be uploaded again: its READY record is gone, so the upload is not `SKIPPED`.

**A new version** (`supersedes`, [2.1](02_discovery.md#21-ingestion)). An upload that names the dataset or the upload it replaces deletes it as part of its own commit: when the last level of the new upload (one stored file) commits READY, one level holds insights and none failed but for having nothing to mine (`EMPTY_LEVEL`: `invalid_schema`, `no_numeric_targets`, `no_candidates`, `no_viable_insights` — a coarser level of too few cells), that batch's transaction checkpoints the journal too and, after the new patterns are appended and have joined their anchors, removes the old datasets the way above (`Engine._remove`: artefacts into the checkpoint, journal rows dropped and renumbered, `ontology.forget`) before the snapshot is derived; the removal is atomic with that level's commit: the READY record is its one commit point, and an error or a crash before it rolls back the batch and the removal together. Between the levels of a new upload both versions are in the graph (each level commits on its own); a crash there leaves the unfinished level `interrupted` and the old version in place, to be replaced by a `PATCH` or deleted. Because the new version joins the anchors first, the anchors it shares with the old keep their ids (on S-parts-v2 both anchors the new part activates existed before it, [10.3](10_verification.md#103-hypothesis-benchmark)), and the graph holds the same pattern ids as one that got the new version at once. The batch record gets `superseded {datasets, batches, patterns_removed, anchors_removed}`; a level that failed for any other reason leaves the old version with a warning (so does an old batch still queued: `busy`), and a last level that commits nothing (`SKIPPED`, or nothing to mine) applies it in a deletion transaction of its own, and the Dataset node carries `supersedes`. `PATCH /api/datasets/{id}` with `supersedes` deletes what it names in the transaction that changes the record: no new batch, no mining. A dataset never replaces itself, its own upload, nor a dataset of its own upload (`PATCH` refuses those with `invalid_options`, and with `busy` a target with a batch still to run). An empty level counts as done, so a new geo upload whose geo levels all have too few cells and whose table level is READY replaces the old geo version too: its insights are the table's alone.

Tests (`test_deletion.py::test_an_interrupted_deletion_is_undone`: an error and a simulated crash both leave the workspace exactly as before; `test_delete_dataset_removes_its_knowledge_and_orphan_anchors`): the dataset's patterns, folder, blocks and records disappear, and its literals leave the literal cache; every surviving anchor has a member or a prior link and every removed one had neither; journal rows, vector rows, `next_chunk_id` and the frame agree; the snapshot equals a fresh rebuild; a question still answers; the dataset can be re-ingested; deleting the last dataset empties the ontology and a fresh ingest cold-starts.

## 6.9 RDF export

`GET /api/export/geosparql.ttl` (`Engine.export_geosparql`, `geosparql.build_turtle`) writes the committed graph as RDF Turtle, for a triple store or any GeoSPARQL tool; Neo4j stays the mirror ([6.6](#66-neo4j-mirror)). It is computed from the snapshot and the stored extents on every request (no cache), and adds no runtime dependency: the Turtle is written as text. A degraded workspace refuses it with 409 `workspace_degraded`, as `/api/search`: an empty file would read as an empty graph to a store synchronising from it.

| Graph | RDF |
|---|---|
| a node | the IRI `urn:sig:<node id>` (`urn:sig:P-…`, `urn:sig:DS:ds-…`, `urn:sig:R:5:…`, `urn:sig:A-3`), the same in every export of one graph |
| Dataset | `sig:Dataset` (a `prov:Entity`): `rdfs:label` the file, `sig:sourceReliability`, `sig:source` when declared, `sig:integrity`, `prov:wasDerivedFrom` its upload (`urn:sig:up-…`, a `prov:Entity`; the upload id is the first 12 hex of the file's SHA-256, the full hash is not exported) |
| Pattern | `sig:Insight` (a `prov:Entity`): `rdfs:label` the headline, `sig:target`, `sig:effectSize`, `sig:support`, `sig:weight`, `sig:pAdjusted` (not on a covariance insight), `sig:credibility`, `sig:integrity`, `prov:wasDerivedFrom` its dataset; non-weak ACTIVATES as `sig:activates`, IN_REGION as `sig:inRegion` (a base pattern's as `sig:inRegionByEvents`, a sub-property: its region holds its events, not its cells) |
| a geo pattern | also a `geo:Feature`: `geo:hasGeometry` a `geo:Geometry` whose `geo:asWKT` is the `MULTIPOLYGON` its cells cover (`spatial.cells_to_wkt`, longitude latitude in CRS84, holes kept; an area across the antimeridian is not split and reads wrong), and one `sig:h3Cell` per cell (the DGGS literal of GeoSPARQL 1.1 is still a placeholder) |
| Region | `sig:Region` and `geo:Feature`: `sig:resolution`, `sig:h3Cell`, its cell as `geo:asWKT`, and `sig:parentRegion` for PARENT_OF |
| Attractor | `skos:Concept` in the scheme `urn:sig:anchors`: `skos:prefLabel` its label, RELATED_TO as `skos:related` |
| CO_LOCATED | `sig:extentsOverlap`, `sig:extentsTouch` or `sig:sameRegion` by its `relation` ([6.1](#61-the-structural-plane)), each a sub-property of `sig:coLocated` |

The `sig:` vocabulary (`urn:sig:vocab#`) is SIG's own. Two GeoSPARQL terms are left out on purpose. `geo:sfWithin` would claim a region lies inside its parent, which H3 cells do not ([6.1](#61-the-structural-plane)). `geo:rcc8*` and `geo:sf*` are defined by DE-9IM on geometries, while CO_LOCATED compares cell sets. No conformance to those relations is claimed. Not exported: the structural edges, weak memberships, CO_OCCURS, the IN_REGION shares, and the Batch, Dimension and Metric nodes. The test parses the file with rdflib and validates it against the GeoSPARQL 1.1 SHACL shapes (`tests/vendor/geosparql_validator.ttl`). It also queries the insights inside a region with SPARQL and reads a pattern's WKT back into exactly its cells.
